Who We Are

A community built on
ancient foundations.

The Kallipgur Coalition Aboriginal Corporation (ICN 11895) is a coalition in formation, registered 22 June 2026 under the CATSI Act 2006. We stand on Yabaru country, Boorloo — grounded in the principles of self-determination, cultural pride, and collective strength.

Our work bridges the wisdom of traditional knowledge with the practical needs of today, ensuring our people have access to services, opportunities, and a strong voice in decisions that affect their lives.

We believe that when community thrives — when culture is celebrated, when families are supported, and when futures are secured — the whole of Country rises with us.

What We Do

Programs built from
the ground up.

Every initiative we run is designed with community members, shaped by Elders, and delivered in a way that respects cultural protocols. Our programs are not services rendered — they are relationships built.

01
Cultural Programs

Cultural Continuity & Language Revival

Preserving and revitalising our languages, ceremonies, art practices, and traditional knowledge. Working alongside Elders to ensure these sacred traditions are documented, taught, and celebrated by future generations.

  • Language documentation & teaching programs
  • Ceremony support & facilitation
  • Intergenerational knowledge transfer
  • Cultural arts & storytelling workshops
02
Health & Wellbeing

Holistic Community Health

Culturally safe health services addressing physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual wellbeing. Community-controlled, community-led, and always grounded in cultural understanding.

03
Youth & Education

Young Leaders & Education Pathways

Supporting Aboriginal young people through school, vocational training, and higher education with mentorship, scholarships, and culturally affirming learning environments.

04
Economic Development

Community Enterprise & Employment

Creating genuine employment and economic pathways for community members through Aboriginal-led enterprises, training programs, and partnerships that keep wealth within community.

05
Housing & Land

Housing Security & Land Connection

Advocating for and providing access to safe, culturally appropriate housing, while strengthening the relationship between our community and the lands that have sustained us for tens of thousands of years.

06
Elders Support

Elder Care & Recognition

Honouring and supporting our Elders — the knowledge holders of our community — with dedicated care services, recognition programs, and ensuring their wisdom is heard and respected.

Our Foundation

Old ways.
New possibilities.

Aboriginal Australians are the custodians of the world's oldest living culture — a rich tapestry of knowledge, law, ceremony, and connection to Country that has sustained communities for over 65,000 years.

At Kallipgur Coalition, we do not see culture as something to be preserved behind glass. It is alive. It breathes. It adapts. And it is the most powerful tool we have for healing, for strength, and for building the future our community deserves.

Governance

Accountable to community.
Transparent by design.

Kallipgur Coalition is governed by a Board of Directors elected from the community. Every decision is made with integrity, accountability, and deep respect for cultural governance principles passed down by our Elders.

Aunty Rosemary Garrawurra

Chairperson

A proud Elder with over 40 years of community leadership. Rosemary's guidance is grounded in traditional law and her lifelong commitment to cultural preservation and community wellbeing.

Uncle David Kallipgur

Deputy Chair & Cultural Advisor

Knowledge holder and cultural custodian. David has dedicated his life to ensuring the transmission of traditional knowledge and the protection of sacred sites and ceremonial practices.

Sandra Munarra

Director — Community Services

Sandra brings extensive experience in Aboriginal health and social services. Her work has transformed how community members access culturally safe support across all life stages.

James Wirritjal

Director — Economic Development

An advocate for Aboriginal economic sovereignty, James works to ensure that economic participation generates real, lasting wealth that stays within and benefits our community.

Our Governance Principles

Cultural Authority

Traditional law and Elders' wisdom are the supreme authority in all cultural matters.

Transparency

Our finances, decisions, and processes are open to community scrutiny at all times.

Accountability

We are answerable to our community members — not government, not donors, not partners.

Community First

Every resource, every partnership, and every decision is evaluated by one question: does this benefit our community?
